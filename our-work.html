Our Work

Our Work

Big ideas, applied with practical rigour, in service of stronger, more resilient communities. Every engagement below was led by Bruce Peever, founder of Baile Mór.

Financial Strategy

  • City of Cornwall

    Long-Term Financial Plan

    Challenge
    The City’s long-term financial plan was out of date, and Council needed an updated view of its capital, operating, staffing, revenue, debt, and reserve needs.
    Approach
    A four-phase engagement with stakeholder interviews, a current-state and gap analysis, a review of financial policies, and an evergreen 10-year forecasting model the City can update each year, with training for staff.
    Result
    A 10-year plan and forecasting model presented to Council, with recommendations for annual plan updates, multi-year budgeting, and stronger reserve building.

  • Township of Wilmot

    Financial assessment and Long-Term Financial Plan

    Challenge
    Slow financial decision-making, turnover in the finance team, delayed reporting, and budget variances had left the Township facing financial instability and mounting pressures.
    Approach
    A financial maturity assessment covering governance, policies, reporting, budgeting, revenues, reserves, debt, development charges, and capital planning, followed by a Long-Term Financial Plan with scenario modelling and a policy review.
    Result
    An evidence-based diagnosis of the root causes of instability, and an evergreen financial model staff can update to test scenarios, weigh debt and reserve impacts, and support Council’s funding decisions.

Governance

  • London Transit Commission

    Governance review

    Challenge
    After an interim change put City Councillors in place as commissioners, the City of London needed a governance model with clear accountability, timely decisions, and alignment with Council priorities.
    Approach
    Working with Dillon Consulting, Bruce engaged more than 25 people across Council, City administration, and the Commission, compared five peer transit organizations, and assessed three governance options.
    Result
    The review found the issue was how governance was carried out, not the model itself. It recommended keeping the commission model and strengthening it through clearer roles, better reporting, and commissioner training, with a phased roadmap and early quick wins.

  • Town of Fort Frances

    Governance review

    Challenge
    The Town wanted to strengthen its governance policies, structures, and processes, including its use of committees, the roles of Council, committees, and staff, and its protocols for in-camera meetings.
    Approach
    A documentation review, an audit of current practice against the Town’s procedural bylaw and the Municipal Act, on-site stakeholder interviews, and a review of governance models used by comparable municipalities.
    Result
    A delegation of authority bylaw, clearly defined roles for Council and its committees, and practical recommendations with an implementation plan, presented to Town Council.

Operational Improvement

  • City of Waterloo

    Organizational review, “2013 Forward”

    Challenge
    The City wanted its structure, staffing, and culture aligned with the community’s future needs, and its operations demonstrably efficient and effective.
    Approach
    A full engagement plan, a workshop program, and an organizational design method to build a new structure for the City.
    Result
    A new organizational structure for efficient service delivery. The City has returned every three years since to reassess it, most recently for its planning and building departments in response to Bills 109 and 23.

  • City of Guelph

    Service rationalization review

    Challenge
    The City wanted to know whether it was delivering services to residents in the most efficient and effective way.
    Approach
    A review of programs and services against the Strategic Plan and legislation, a value-for-money analysis, and benchmarking against comparator municipalities.
    Result
    Recommendations on future service levels, services that were redundant or no longer delivered public value, and ways to deliver services more cost-effectively across the organization. Council adopted and implemented the recommendations.

  • Region of Waterloo

    Conducted a service delivery review identifying opportunities for improved service delivery and cost reduction across municipal programs and operations.
