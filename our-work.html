Our Work

Big ideas, applied with practical rigour, in service of stronger, more resilient communities. Every engagement below was led by Bruce Peever, founder of Baile Mór.

Financial Strategy

  • City of Cornwall

    Long-Term Financial Plan · 2025

    Challenge
    The City’s last long-term financial plan dated from 2017. Council needed an updated view of its capital, operating, staffing, revenue, debt, and reserve needs.
    Approach
    A four-phase engagement with stakeholder interviews, a current-state and gap analysis, a review of financial policies, and an evergreen 10-year forecasting model the City can update each year, with training for staff.
    Result
    A 10-year plan and forecasting model presented to Council, with recommendations for annual plan updates, multi-year budgeting, and stronger reserve building.

  • Cape Breton Regional Municipality

    Financial management and performance review · 2025–2026

    Challenge
    CBRM had been rated high risk for fiscal instability for three years running. The Nova Scotia Department of Municipal Affairs, with the support of CBRM Council, asked for an independent review.
    Approach
    On-site interviews, a review of policies, budgets, debt, reserves, and tax arrears, workshops with the Department and CBRM, and a financial maturity model covering governance, cash management, revenue, debt and reserves, and organizational capacity.
    Result
    Practical recommendations to strengthen oversight, finance capacity, cash and debt management, reserves, and revenue collection, and to put a long-term financial plan in place, with a phased three-year implementation plan. The recommendations are now under consideration by the Province.

Governance

  • London Transit Commission

    Governance review · 2025–2026

    Challenge
    After a March 2025 interim change put City Councillors in place as commissioners, the City of London needed a governance model with clear accountability, timely decisions, and alignment with Council priorities.
    Approach
    Working with Dillon Consulting, Bruce engaged more than 25 people across Council, City administration, and the Commission, compared five peer transit organizations, and assessed three governance options.
    Result
    The review found the issue was how governance was carried out, not the model itself. It recommended keeping the commission model and strengthening it through clearer roles, better reporting, and commissioner training, with a phased roadmap and early quick wins.

  • City of Windsor

    Agencies, Boards & Commissions governance review

    Challenge
    More than 65 agencies, boards, and committees, a governance model that lacked clarity and consistency, and no formal reporting between committees.
    Approach
    Forty stakeholder interviews and four focus groups, then three governance options, with an Executive Committee model recommended to strengthen strategic alignment and accountability.
    Result
    Advisory committees reduced from 23 to 14, a clear reporting line from every ABC to Council, and a biweekly Council meeting schedule. All recommendations were implemented.

Operational Improvement

  • City of Waterloo

    Organizational review, “2013 Forward” · 2012–2013, refreshed since

    Challenge
    The City wanted its structure, staffing, and culture aligned with the community’s future needs, and its operations demonstrably efficient and effective.
    Approach
    A full engagement plan, a workshop program, and an organizational design method to build a new structure for the City.
    Result
    A new organizational structure for efficient service delivery. The City has returned every three years since to reassess it, most recently in 2023 for its planning and building departments in response to Bills 109 and 23.

  • City of Guelph

    Service rationalization review · 2021

    Challenge
    The City wanted to know whether it was delivering services to residents in the most efficient and effective way.
    Approach
    A review of programs and services against the Strategic Plan and legislation, a value-for-money analysis, and benchmarking against comparator municipalities.
    Result
    Recommendations on future service levels, services that were redundant or no longer delivered public value, and ways to deliver services more cost-effectively across the organization. Council adopted and implemented the recommendations.

  • Region of Waterloo

    Conducted a service delivery review identifying opportunities for improved service delivery and cost reduction across municipal programs and operations.
