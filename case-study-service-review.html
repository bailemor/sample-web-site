Twenty Shared Priorities From More Than a Hundred Ideas: A Service Review for a County and Its Seven Member Municipalities

Client
An upper-tier county in Southwestern Ontario and its seven member municipalities
Sector and location
Municipal government, Ontario
Size
About 850 County employees, plus seven lower-tier towns and townships
Services
Service delivery review, service profiles, benchmarking, opportunity identification, implementation priorities
Timeframe
July to November 2019, a five-month review delivered on schedule and on budget
Led by
Bruce Peever, FCMC

The Challenge

The County and its seven member municipalities needed evidence on where sharing, redesigning or changing services could reduce costs without hurting the customer’s experience.

In 2019 the Province gave small and rural municipalities one-time funding to modernize service delivery and slow the growth of spending, and it expected local governments to find efficiencies. Each member municipality delivered its own services, with its own facilities and its own service levels. Long-term care, libraries, solid waste and social services were outside the review’s scope.

The Approach

The review ran in five phases under a Project Charter agreed with the County’s Project Team and Steering Committee.

  1. Initiate Project initiation
  2. Scan Environmental scan
  3. Assess Current service delivery review
  4. Identify Opportunity identification
  5. Report Final report
  1. Understand how services run today. We reviewed documents and interviewed the CAO and department heads in the County and in each member municipality. We built service profiles for every municipality, so everyone worked from the same picture of the current model.
  2. Compare against peers. We benchmarked performance against five comparator municipalities chosen by the County and its members, using financial statements and Financial Information Return data, to find where results differed most.
  3. Generate opportunities. In working sessions with the Steering Committee, we used a framework of new public sector delivery models to find ways to eliminate or transfer services, recover more of their cost, redesign them, deliver them differently or change service levels. The sessions produced more than a hundred ideas.
  4. Score and rank. The County’s project team scored each opportunity against seven criteria: operating impact, capital impact, barriers to implementation, recent reviews, comparator results, strategic fit and customer impact. The highest-ranked and most transformational opportunities became the “Top 20 in ’20” list.

The Results

The County and its member municipalities received a ranked list of twenty shared opportunities, each with observations and leading practices, and a fact base for every municipality.

What the Top 20 cover

  • Shared operations: shared public works facilities, consistent facility management, coordinated winter maintenance across municipal boundaries, sale of surplus buildings and a county-wide approach to municipal drains.
  • Shared expertise: shared specialists such as chief building officials, clerks and fire chiefs, coordinated project management for large capital projects, coordinated HR services and lean management.
  • Money: group purchasing, a county-wide investment strategy for cash, debt and reserves, and a county-wide user fee study.
  • Planning and growth: one Official Plan for the county, a shared land inventory and GIS data, a county-wide asset management approach, coordinated economic development and a shared approach to climate change and energy efficiency.
  • Digital and communication: common IT systems toward a “Digital County”, shared communications services and faster approval processes.

A report for every municipality

Each member municipality received its own report with service profiles and financial, staffing and service benchmarks. One town’s report, for example, drew on interviews across eight service areas and ten years of Financial Information Return data. It found that the town’s total reserves had grown from 9% to 25% of the book value of its capital assets, and that about 13% of its full-time staff could retire with unreduced pensions within five years, a chance to realign roles as positions turn over.

The report recommended the priority opportunities for implementation in a second phase.

Lessons for Other Municipalities

  • Look across boundaries Most of the twenty priorities came from sharing services between municipalities, not from cuts inside one of them.
  • Let the client do the scoring Staff scored every opportunity against agreed criteria, so the ranking reflected local judgement.
  • Give every partner its own facts A profile and benchmarks for each municipality let every council see where it stood.
