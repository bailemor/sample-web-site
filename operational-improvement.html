Operational Improvement

Objective, evidence-based reviews of structure, staffing and service delivery, with a roadmap leadership and Council can act on with confidence.

You suspect something isn’t working. We give you the evidence and the roadmap. This is the practice area where Baile Mór’s founder has the deepest track record: hundreds of engagements over more than three decades, ranging from small rural townships to some of Canada’s largest cities, universities, and police services.

Every organization eventually faces the same question: are we structured, staffed, and delivering services the way our community actually needs? Answering that well requires more than instinct. It requires an objective, evidence-based assessment.

Representative work

Engagements led by Bruce Peever, founder of Baile Mór. For the full stories, read the case studies on the organizational review of a Northern Ontario city and the shared services review for a city and its police service.

  • A growing city’s public library

    New main library business case

    Challenge
    The library’s half-century-old main branch no longer met community needs. It lacked space for programs, seating, and meetings, needed major building-system upgrades, and fell short of building code and accessibility requirements. Council asked for a business case before committing to a new main library within a larger downtown redevelopment.
    Approach
    A five-phase engagement with stakeholder interviews, a current-state and gap analysis, space-needs forecasts to 2036 using recognized library benchmarks, a survey of six comparator main libraries, a risk assessment, capital and operating cost estimates, and an economic impact analysis.
    Result
    A business case, presented to the Library Board and Council, that sized the new main library at almost three times the current building and set out its costs, economic benefits, and risks. City staff then recommended that Council confirm the size and fund preliminary design. The project went ahead: the net-zero library, more than two and a half times the size of the old branch, is set to open in 2027 as the anchor of the downtown redevelopment, with a makerspace, recording studios, a teen area, and bookable rooms for groups of two to 100.

  • A large Ontario regional municipality

    Service review update

    Challenge
    The Region had made good progress on the 63 opportunities from its 2015 service review. With provincial Audit and Accountability Fund support, and with less provincial grant funding expected, it wanted an independent update focused on discretionary services and on services where it cost more than its peers.
    Approach
    A five-phase, four-month engagement in 2019. Working sessions with staff in every department updated the service profiles, classed each service as mandatory, essential, traditional, or discretionary, and traced each service level to its source in legislation, Council direction, management, or funding agreements. The Region was then benchmarked against six large Ontario peers, and each opportunity was rated with a Disruption Gauge that weighs external and internal impact, risk, and strategic alignment.
    Result
    Nineteen top opportunities to pursue or study further, each with a savings range, an implementation timeline, and a disruption rating, plus three grant options and five leading-practice workshop topics for Council. The largest potential savings were in managing and maintaining Regional buildings, maintaining social housing assets, discretionary employee benefits, and directly operated child care. The report found the Region to be a high-performing organization with few easy wins left, and recommended the opportunities to inform budgets from 2021.
