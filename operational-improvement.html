Operational Improvement

Objective, evidence-based reviews of structure, staffing and service delivery, with a roadmap leadership and Council can act on with confidence.

You suspect something isn’t working. We give you the evidence and the roadmap. This is the practice area where Baile Mór’s founder has the deepest track record: hundreds of engagements over more than three decades, ranging from small rural townships to some of Canada’s largest cities, universities, and police services.

Every organization eventually faces the same question: are we structured, staffed, and delivering services the way our community actually needs? Answering that well requires more than instinct. It requires an objective, evidence-based assessment.

Representative work

Engagements led by Bruce Peever, founder of Baile Mór. For the full story of another, read the organizational review of a Northern Ontario city case study.

  • A growing city’s public library

    New main library business case

    Challenge
    The library’s half-century-old main branch no longer met community needs. It lacked space for programs, seating, and meetings, needed major building-system upgrades, and fell short of building code and accessibility requirements. Council asked for a business case before committing to a new main library within a larger downtown redevelopment.
    Approach
    A five-phase engagement with stakeholder interviews, a current-state and gap analysis, space-needs forecasts to 2036 using recognized library benchmarks, a survey of six comparator main libraries, a risk assessment, capital and operating cost estimates, and an economic impact analysis.
    Result
    A business case, presented to the Library Board and Council, that sized the new main library at almost three times the current building and set out its costs, economic benefits, and risks. City staff then recommended that Council confirm the size and fund preliminary design.

  • A large Ontario regional municipality

    Service review update

    Challenge
    With provincial Audit and Accountability Fund support, the Region wanted to update its earlier service delivery review and find realistic, practical ways to deliver services more efficiently and effectively, at lower cost.
    Approach
    An update of the Region’s service profiles, with each service classed as mandatory, essential, traditional, or discretionary, and a comparison with peer municipalities to find the traditional and discretionary services where the Region’s costs differed.
    Result
    Twenty opportunities to change service levels and delivery models, presented for Regional Council’s consideration.

  • A Northern Ontario city and its police service

    Shared services feasibility study

    Challenge
    The City and its Police Services Board needed to ease pressure on taxes while keeping or improving service levels. A federal order to move to Next Generation 911 also meant the Fire Department would need a new dispatch system.
    Approach
    A review of finance, human resources, procurement, and dispatch across the City, Police Service, and Fire Department, with interviews and working sessions with 13 stakeholders, a current-state comparison of processes, staffing, systems, and costs, and an analysis of fire call volumes.
    Result
    Eight core recommendations and five further opportunities, each ranked for complexity and impact. They included four quick wins and a shared police and fire dispatch centre that would split the cost of the new system and free firefighters for active duty.
